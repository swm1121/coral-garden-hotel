The Island

What to expect
before you arrive.

Rota is a small island — about 33 square miles — in the Northern Mariana Islands chain, roughly 37 miles northeast of Guam. Population 1,893 (2020 census). Primarily limestone and karst terrain, with coastal cliffs, cave systems, and a reef shelf that drops into the Philippine Sea. One paved loop road covers most of the island.

There are no cruise ships, no resort corridors, and no peak-season crowds. What there is: some of the clearest water in the Pacific, a resident diving community, good hiking, and a genuinely unhurried pace. Travelers who have been to Guam, Saipan, or Hawaii and found them overrun tend to find Rota exactly right.

WiFi is available at the hotel. Rental cars are available at the airport — book in advance, as inventory on-island is limited. Most things a traveler needs in Songsong are within walking distance of Coral Garden.

What to Do

Scuba Diving

Rota's water clarity is exceptional — visibility commonly runs 80 to 150 feet. Named dive sites include Coral Gardens reef, the Shoun Maru wreck (a WWII-era Japanese cargo vessel now heavily encrusted with coral), and Senhanom Point wall. Two operators are based in Songsong, both within walking distance of the hotel.

Snorkeling

Coral Gardens is the standout — clear water, healthy reef, sea turtles, and calm conditions that make it reliable for all levels. Both dive operators run snorkeling trips. Direct beach access is also available without arranging anything in advance.

Beaches & Swimming

Teteto Beach won Best Beach Overseas at the Marine Diving Fair in Tokyo three consecutive years (2018–2020). Also worth visiting: Tweksberry Beach and West Harbor. All uncrowded, all genuinely clear.

Swimming Hole

One of the most-visited spots on the island among locals and returning visitors. A natural freshwater swimming area in a serene setting. Unhurried and easy to spend an afternoon at.

Tonga Cave

A large natural cave a short walk from Songsong Village. Stalactites and stalagmites throughout. During World War II the cave was used as a Japanese military hospital. Worth the short walk from the hotel.

Taga Latte Stone Quarry

The most impressive latte stone quarry in the Marianas. Nine large shafts and seven capstones left in various stages of the ancient Chamorro quarrying process. A significant and unhurried cultural site.

Bird Sanctuary

Rota's Bird Sanctuary is home to the Rota White-eye, a small bird found nowhere else on Earth. The island's low traffic and intact forested interior make it one of the better places in the Pacific for serious bird watching.

Viewpoints & Scenic Spots

Songsong Lookout offers an elevated view over the village and the water below. Alaguan Bay overlook, Pona Point, Wedding Cake Mountain, and As Matmos — a dramatic sea cliff that hosts the annual Cliff Fishing Derby — are all worth the short drives.

Getting Here

By Air

Rota is served by Benjamin Taisacan Manglona International Airport (ROP). There is no direct service from the US mainland — a connection through Guam or Saipan is required. Star Marianas Air and MACS (Micronesian Air Charter Services) operate regional service. Schedules change frequently; confirm close to your travel date.

From Guam & Saipan

Guam (GUM) is the main international gateway with the most connections from the US mainland and Asia. Saipan (SPN) is also an option. Both have multiple weekly flights to Rota. Charter service is available with advance notice.

On the Island

The airport is about 15 minutes from Songsong by car. There are no taxis — a rental car is the practical option. Two rental companies operate on-island: Islander Rent-A-Car (670) 532-0901 and NAAI Car Rental (670) 532-6225. Book before arrival — inventory is limited. Within Songsong, most things guests need are walkable from the hotel.

Practical Notes for Travelers

Cash. There is one ATM on the island — Bank of Guam. Bring enough for your stay, especially over long weekends when the bank is closed.

Passport. US citizens don't legally need a passport for the CNMI. Bring one anyway — some connecting airport staff don't recognize the CNMI as US territory and may create problems without it.

Medications. The pharmacy at Rota Health Center carries limited stock. Bring everything you need.

Season. Best weather and dive conditions run December through May. Typhoon risk is highest June through November.

Divers. The nearest hyperbaric chamber is on Guam. DAN membership is worth having before you travel.
